What LS Swap Parts Actually Cost

Endicott Speed Shop · Green car build log · Prices as of April 2026

The advertised price is rarely the real price. Here's a documented look at what a basic LS1/T5 swap into a second-gen F-body costs once you add every required supporting part.

Prices are as listed in April 2026 and will change. Part numbers and prices are from our own orders and the vendors' pages at the time.

We're building a 1977 Firebird Formula into a running LS-swapped development car. The engine is a 2004 GTO LS1 that came out of a running car. The transmission is a T5 from an '84 Z28. The car was originally a 4-speed car, so the manual pedal setup was already in place. This should be about as straightforward as an LS swap gets.

So we built a parts list. Then we started ordering. Here's what we found.

The oil pan: $270 becomes $760

The Holley 302-2 LS swap oil pan is the standard recommendation for fitting an LS into a second-gen F-body with the stock subframe. It was listed at $499.95 — well above the ~$270 we'd estimated from older forum references. And the pan is only the beginning: it doesn't include a gasket or a filter, and it needs a specific dipstick tube and dipstick because the swap pan moves the dipstick location.

The dipstick tube is the number that catches people off guard: $120 for what is essentially a bent tube with a seal. But the stock tube doesn't reach the new pan location without modification.

The headers: $300 becomes $463

Speed Engineering's 1-3/4" F-body LS swap headers were listed at $299.99. The product notes say the headers are pulled in very tight for frame clearance and require their own spark plug wires — and in our car, the GTO's stock wires would not clear. Head-bolt and gasket upgrades aren't strictly required but are the right choice for a lasting installation.

The clutch system: $80 becomes $359

We're running a hydraulic throwout bearing instead of a mechanical clutch fork, which avoids the Z-bar fabrication and the header clearance problems. The McLeod 1434005 kit for 1970–81 F-bodies includes the master cylinder, firewall bracket, braided line and hardware. Our ~$80 estimate assumed a master cylinder alone; the full kit is $359. The Howe Racing 82876 hydraulic throwout bearing for the GM T5 is a separate $152.99 purchase — the McLeod kit does not include a bearing.

The overrun is concentrated in four systems: oil pan, clutch kit, bellhousing and header accessories. Each looked like a small difference on its own. Together they added over $1,000 before the wiring harness, fuel system, cooling or suspension work began.

What to take from this

None of these were surprises in hindsight. The pan's extra parts are documented if you read carefully; the header maker says its wires are required; the clutch kit is a more complete system than a bare master cylinder; and the bellhousing estimate came from an out-of-date price.

These are the right parts for the job. The lesson is that estimates built from headline prices run systematically low. On any system-level purchase — oil pan, headers, clutch, cooling — budget roughly 40% over the base price for the required supporting parts.
