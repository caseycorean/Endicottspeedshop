* Change in the car's resistance to rotating about a vertical axis (yaw, or polar, moment of inertia), measured about the car's center of gravity before and after. It treats each listed part as a point weight that moves without rotating, and includes the effect of the center of gravity itself shifting. Negative means less yaw inertia, which helps the car change direction — but how quickly it actually responds also depends on the tires, suspension and the turning force available. See why that matters more than 50/50.

How it works

Each part's weight times its distance from the front axle is a moment. Removing a part takes its moment away; adding one puts a moment in; moving one does both. The new center of gravity is the total moment divided by the new total weight; front weight follows from where that center sits between the axles. The same arithmetic gives side-to-side balance and, when you supply a starting height, CG height.

Part weights marked measured were weighed by ESS (see what each includes). Locations in the presets are typical estimates for a second-gen F-body — edit them for your car. Measured weights include different hardware (for example, the stock subframe was weighed on its wheels and the C5 assembly without), so don't subtract one preset from another unless they include the same parts. A blank Y means on the centerline; a blank Z means at the car's CG height.
